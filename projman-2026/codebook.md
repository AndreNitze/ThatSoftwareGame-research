# Twelve-code frame for the ProjMAN formative design case

The original inductive frame was developed from the archive, including contextual material. Definitions and code assignments are retained in the corrected report; source roles and frequency bases are separated. Multiple coding is permitted when an existing meaning unit expresses distinct concerns. The English aliases below map directly to the original stable code names. The subsequent grouping into three requirements is interpretive, overlapping, and not a partition of all codes.

### Orientation — `ONBOARDING_ORIENTIERUNG`

**Definition:** Understandable entry, objectives, first actions, and contextual guidance.

**Include:** Tutorials, guides, trial rounds, goal explanations, next-action cues, and initial uncertainty.

**Exclude:** Ongoing detail visibility without an entry/guidance concern belongs to information transparency.

**Primary example:** SRC-045, SEG-0638:

> Es sollte am Anfang besser erklärt werden, was man machen muss.

**Reach:** 111 primary segments; 65 questionnaire records; 13 maps.

### Pacing/time control — `TEMPO_ZEITSTEUERUNG`

**Definition:** Perceived simulation speed and the ability to stop or regulate time.

**Include:** Fast days, pause, speed controls, reading modes, and phase-based play.

**Exclude:** Time management solely as PM subject matter or missing project-duration information.

**Primary example:** SRC-017, SEG-0016:

> Zu schneller Tagesablauf (zu wenig Zeit)

**Reach:** 46 primary segments; 22 questionnaire records; 11 maps.

### Interface/usability — `UI_BEDIENBARKEIT`

**Definition:** Interaction, navigation, and visual interface presentation.

**Include:** Buttons, icons, layout, mobile use, drag-and-drop, language, animation, and simultaneous views.

**Exclude:** Missing substantive project/employee information belongs to information transparency.

**Primary example:** SRC-020, SEG-0055:

> Projektmarkt als Button

**Reach:** 104 primary segments; 61 questionnaire records; 13 maps.

### Information transparency — `INFORMATION_TRANSPARENZ`

**Definition:** Visibility and intelligibility of decision-relevant states, consequences, and feedback.

**Include:** Project/staff details, cost, duration, utilization, consequences, failure reasons, reports, and result feedback.

**Exclude:** General introductory guidance without a specific information concern.

**Primary example:** SRC-032, SEG-0234:

> Konsequenzen der Handlungen nicht deutlich genug

**Reach:** 72 primary segments; 20 questionnaire records; 13 maps.

### Technical reliability — `TECHNISCHE_ZUVERLAESSIGKEIT`

**Definition:** Stability, consistent states, and functioning game operations.

**Include:** Crashes, connection problems, reset/save failures, markets, XP, funds, recruiting, and progress faults.

**Exclude:** Mistakes explained solely by unclear instructions.

**Primary example:** SRC-045, SEG-0381:

> Belastabrkeit, bei zu vielen Spielern stürzt es ab

**Reach:** 52 primary segments; 29 questionnaire records; 7 maps.

### Mechanics/progression — `SPIELMECHANIK_PROGRESSION`

**Definition:** Game loop, resource economy, personnel mechanics, and progression.

**Include:** Project choice, levels/skills, recruiting, training, budget, reputation, unlocks, rewards, and new game functions.

**Exclude:** PM topics as learning content rather than proposed game mechanics.

**Primary example:** SRC-025, SEG-0099:

> Level-System

**Reach:** 256 primary segments; 72 questionnaire records; 17 maps.

### PM content/realism — `PM_INHALTE_REALISMUS`

**Definition:** Project-management concepts and realistic consequences as game subject matter.

**Include:** Project phases, risk, quality, staffing, cost/time management, roles, strategy, trade-offs, and long-term effects.

**Exclude:** Purely technical or visual changes without disciplinary substance.

**Primary example:** SRC-025, SEG-0119:

> Kompromisse statt Ideallösungen

**Reach:** 118 primary segments; 16 questionnaire records; 12 maps.

### Difficulty/support — `SCHWIERIGKEIT_UNTERSTUETZUNG`

**Definition:** Adaptation of challenge and support to prior knowledge and perceived load.

**Include:** Difficulty levels, easy entry, help, hints, avatars/chatbots, manuals, and overload.

**Exclude:** General introduction without an adaptive/support concern.

**Primary example:** SRC-043, SEG-0310:

> Handbuch zum Nachschauen

**Reach:** 28 primary segments; 14 questionnaire records; 6 maps.

### Pedagogical integration/reflection — `DIDAKTISCHE_EINBETTUNG_REFLEXION`

**Definition:** Connections with courses, supporting learning activities, and reflection.

**Include:** Modules, preparation/review, quizzes, interviews, debriefing, learning-progress displays, and prerequisites.

**Exclude:** Formal grading belongs to assessment; game mechanics without a teaching connection are excluded.

**Primary example:** SRC-028, SEG-0192:

> Add a post-project review screen showing what went well, what failed, and why.

**Reach:** 49 primary segments; 7 questionnaire records; 11 maps.

### Cooperation — `KOOPERATION`

**Definition:** Mechanisms or incentives for cooperative or competitive multiplayer activity.

**Include:** Shared projects/resources, employee exchange, group play, rankings, and cooperation incentives.

**Exclude:** Personnel/team management solely within one individual player company.

**Primary example:** SRC-032, SEG-0248:

> Ressourcen kombinieren, um größere Projekte übernehmen zu können - besserer Fortschritt

**Reach:** 11 primary segments; 1 questionnaire records; 6 maps.

### Assessment — `BEWERTUNG`

**Definition:** Use of game results or activity in grades and formal assessment.

**Include:** Grades, bonus credit, optional assessment alternatives, and validity concerns.

**Exclude:** In-game rewards without a course-assessment connection.

**Primary example:** SRC-017, SEG-0023:

> Keine Einbeziehung in Bewertung

**Reach:** 7 primary segments; 1 questionnaire records; 5 maps.

### Experience/acceptance — `SPIELERLEBNIS_AKZEPTANZ`

**Definition:** Positive or negative overall experience, appeal, enjoyment, and acceptance.

**Include:** Enjoyment, appealing design, intuitive/simple operation, realism, motivation, rejection, and lack of excitement.

**Exclude:** Specific isolated problems without an overall experiential evaluation.

**Primary example:** SRC-045, SEG-0599:

> Die selbsterklärende Bedienoberfläche.

**Reach:** 167 primary segments; 111 questionnaire records; 7 maps.
